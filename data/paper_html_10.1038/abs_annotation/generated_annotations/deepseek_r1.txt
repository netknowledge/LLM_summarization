An electrokinetic sorting method using dielectrophoresis (DEP) in microfluidic channels was developed to isolate rare cells from complex mixtures with high efficiency. By labeling target cells with particles that modulate their dielectrophoretic response, the approach enables continuous-flow separation of labeled and unlabeled cells under engineered electric fields. Demonstration through enrichment of rare marker-expressing bacteria achieved over 200-fold enrichment at a throughput of 10,000 cells per second, highlighting DACS's potential for high-throughput, high-purity, and efficient rare cell recovery in a disposable format.

The study investigates how different levels of Hedgehog signaling in Drosophila wings are translated into distinct cellular responses. By using chimeric and deleted forms of the Smoothened receptor, researchers demonstrated that low and high Hedgehog signals are mediated by different configurations of a regulatory complex involving Costal and Fused proteins. Low signaling is initiated when an inhibitory site on the Smoothened cytoplasmic tail shifts the complex to its low state, while high signaling requires activation by multiple Smoothened tails. This dual mechanism allows precise implementation of Hedgehog morphogen gradients, leading to distinct intervein and vein fates in wing development.

The structure of *Escherichia coli* succinate dehydrogenase (SQR) reveals its electron transport pathway from succinate to ubiquinone. The arrangement of redox centers minimizes reactive oxygen species (ROS) formation at FAD, explaining why SQR is preferred during aerobic respiration over fumarate reductase, which generates high ROS levels. This finding links mitochondrial SQR mutations in genetic disorders to ROS-induced symptoms due to impaired electron transport.

This study evaluates the impact of electronic health record–based clinical decision support (CDS) and physician performance feedback on adherence to acute otitis media and otitis media with effusion treatment guidelines. Conducted as a cluster randomized trial across 24 primary care practices, it analyzed data from 139,305 patient visits. Results showed that CDS significantly improved comprehensive guideline adherence for both conditions, particularly enhancing pain management and appropriate antibiotic use. While performance feedback also contributed to better prescribing practices, combining CDS with feedback did not yield additive benefits. Notably, variability in CDS utilization across practices ranged widely, suggesting the need for strategies to enhance system adoption. The findings underscore the effectiveness of CDS and feedback as standalone interventions in improving guideline compliance, though their combined use does not offer additional advantages.

The study investigates the role of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (Ah) in fetal vascular development. Mice lacking a functional Ah gene exhibit developmental abnormalities, including smaller livers and reduced body weights. Analysis reveals that liver size reduction is due to smaller hepatocytes caused by significant portosystemic shunting, with 56% of portal blood bypassing the liver sinusoids. This shunting is linked to a patent ductus venosus in adults and persistence of fetal vascular structures. Additionally, intravital microscopy shows immature liver sinusoidal architecture and persistent hyaloid arteries in the eyes, while corrosion casting highlights vascular abnormalities in the kidneys. These findings highlight Ah receptor’s role beyond xenobiotic metabolism, specifically in resolving fetal vascular structures during development.
A study utilizing Cre-lox fate mapping in transgenic mice reveals that a small subpopulation of oligodendrocytes originates from the Dbx1-expressing domain of the ventricular zone, spanning the dorsoventral midline. These Dbx-derived oligodendrocytes constitute less than 5% of the total, are generated late during embryogenesis through radial glial transformation, and primarily localize to the lateral white matter. Additionally, Dbx-expressing precursors contribute to astrocytes and interneurons but not to the postnatal ependymal layer.
